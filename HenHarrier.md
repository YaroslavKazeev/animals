# Hen harrier

## Circus cyaneus (LA) / Blauwe kiekendief (NL) / Полевой лунь (RU)

![Hen harrier](https://www.earlham.ac.uk/sites/default/files/styles/image_3c_desktop_1_5x/public/images/articles/Circus%20of%20life%20a%20tale%20of%20two%20harriers/circus-of-life-tale-of-two-harriers-diagram.jpg?itok=alq0Txg8)

### Description

The hen harrier is a bird of prey. The term "hen harrier" refers to its former habit of preying on free-ranging fowl.

The hen harrier has a population of 330.000-512.000. The Hen Harrier nests in the Northern Hemisphere from the forest-tundra in the North to the steppe zone in the South. In Eurasia, it is distributed along the entire length from west to east.

It inhabits mainly open landscapes. In the forest zone, it is found on forest edges, clearings, burnt-out areas, moss bogs, on the outskirts of fields, in river valley meadows. During the nesting period, it gives greatest preference to small clearings 3-5 years old, densely overgrown with nettles, fireweed, and raspberry bushes

It migrates to more southerly areas in winter. Eurasian birds move to southern Europe and southern temperate Asia. In the mildest regions, such as France and Great Britain, hen harriers may be present all year, but the higher ground is largely deserted in winter.

The hen harrier is 41–52 cm long with a 97–122 cm wingspan. The sexes also differ in weight, with males weighing on average 350 g and females weighing on average 530 g.

The male is mainly grey above and white below except for the upper breast, which is grey like the upperparts, and the rump, which is white; the wings are grey with black wingtips. The female is brown above with white upper tail coverts.
