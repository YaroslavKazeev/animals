# Eurasian sparrowhawk

## Accipiter nisus (LA) / Sperwer (NL) / Ястреб-перепелятник (RU)

![Eurasian sparrowhawk image](https://cdn.download.ams.birds.cornell.edu/api/v1/asset/254927951/1800)

### Description

The Eurasian sparrowhawk is a small bird of prey with short, broad wings and a long tail, which helps it maneuver through the trees. The resemblance of the common cuckoo to the Eurasian sparrowhawk helps it avoid aggression from the small birds whose nest it seeks to parasitize.

The Eurasian sparrowhawk has a population of 1.500.000. It is distributed in Europe, from Western Asia to Siberia and Iran. In winter, the northern population moves south to the Mediterranean, northeast Africa, Saudi Arabia, and Pakistan.

Females are larger by 25% than males, which is a common sexual demorphism in birds of prey. The adult female reaches a wingspan of 67–80 cm, a length of 35–41 cm, and a weight of 186–345 grams. The adult male reaches a wingspan of 59–64 cm and a length of 29–34 cm.

Both partners are dark gray, but some acquire a bluish tint. The underside has pale gray stripes and a reddish tint, and when viewed from a distance, the bird has a rufous color.

Usually a silent bird outside the breeding season. The main call is a shrill cackle "kek-hek-kek-kek…" or "kokekekekek…": slower in contact between members of a pair, faster when threatening an intruder, a person, or another predator. The softer "kewkew-kew-hew…" is used when bringing prey to the nest and during courtship.

According to Greek mythology, Nisus, the king of Megara, was turned into a sparrowhawk after his daughter, Scylla, cut off his purple lock of hair to present to her lover (and Nisus' enemy), Minos. The Old Norse name for the Eurasian sparrowhawk, sparrhaukr, was thought to have been coined by Vikings who encountered falconry in England.
