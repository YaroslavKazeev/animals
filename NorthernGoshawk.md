## Accipiter gentilis (LA) / Havik (NL) / Ястреб-тетеревятник (RU)

### Description

The Northern goshawk has a population of 500.000. They live in mountainous and forested areas of Eurasia and North America. They inhabit coniferous and deciduous forests. They prefer to build nests in forests that consist of old and tall trees with average lighting and small forest edges nearby for hunting.

In July 2023, the Northern goshawk was split into two species based on significant morphological and genetic differences: Eurasian goshawk and American goshawk.

The Eurasian goshawk is the largest species of the genus Accipiter. Females have a 105–115 cm wingspan, are about 61 cm long, and weigh 860–1600 g. Males have a wingspan of 98–104 cm, are about 55 cm long, and weigh 630–1100 g.

All hawks, including the goshawk, have characteristic white stripes of feathers above the eyes, which create the impression of white eyebrows. In the goshawk, they are wider and longer than in other representatives of the genus, and almost meet at the back of the head.

The coloration of both males and females varies from bluish-grey to black. The back, head, and wing coverts are usually darker; the underparts are light with distinct grey transverse ripples. The tail is light grey with three or four dark stripes. Juveniles are markedly different in color from adults: their upperparts, head, and outer wing parts are brown, and the breast is white with vertical brown stripes.
